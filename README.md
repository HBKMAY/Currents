## 📲 Installing Currents on Your Phone

Once the download finishes, let's get the app onto your device. This is straightforward, even if you've never installed an app outside the Google Play Store.

### 🔓 Allow Unknown Sources (One-Time Setup)

1. Open your phone's **Settings**.
2. Go to **Security** or **Privacy** (this varies by phone model).
3. Find the option labeled **"Install unknown apps"** or **"Allow from this source"**.
4. Select your browser (like Chrome or Firefox) and toggle **"Allow from this source"** to **ON**.

*Why?* Because you're downloading directly from a trusted developer, not the Play Store, Android asks for your permission first. This is a standard safety measure.

### 🛠️ Install the App

1. Open your **Downloads** folder or tap the notification that appeared when the download completed.
2. Tap the `Currents` APK file.
3. A prompt will appear asking for confirmation—tap **"Install"**.
4. Wait a few seconds for the magic to happen.
5. Tap **"Open"** to launch Currents for the first time.

**Congratulations!** You're now ready to browse Reddit like a pro.

## 🔑 Connect Your Reddit Account

Currents respects your privacy and security. To use the app, you'll need a Reddit **Client ID**. This might sound technical, but it's a simple process that takes less than two minutes.

### What's a Client ID?

Reddit requires all third-party apps to have a unique identifier. It's like a key that lets Currents talk to Reddit on your behalf. You get this key from Reddit itself, and it's completely free.

### How to Get Your Client ID (Step-by-Step)

1. Open a web browser on your computer or phone and go to **[reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com)**.
2. Log in to your Reddit account (or create one if you don't have one—it's free!).
3. Click your profile icon in the top-right corner and select **"User Settings"**.
4. Scroll down and click the **"Safety & Privacy"** tab.
5. Look for the section labeled **"Manage third-party app authorization"** and click **"Go to developer settings"**.
6. On the developer page, click the **"Create App"** or **"Create Another App"** button.
7. Fill out the form:
   - **Name:** Call it anything you like (e.g., "My Currents App").
   - **Type:** Select **"Installed app"** (important!).
   - **Description:** Optional—leave it blank or add a note.
   - **About URL:** Leave blank.
   - **Redirect URI:** Enter `http://localhost` (exactly like this).
8. Click **"Create App"**.
9. You'll see a new entry appear. Look for a string of random letters and numbers under the app name—that's your **Client ID** (it looks like a long code, something like `aBcDeFgHiJkLmN`).
10. Copy that code.

### ⚙️ Enter Your Client ID in Currents

1. Open Currents on your phone.
2. On the welcome screen, paste (or type) your Client ID into the designated field.
3. Tap **"Continue"** or **"Sign In"**.
4. Currents will ask you to grant permission to your Reddit account—tap **"Authorize"**.
5. You're in! Enjoy your personalized Reddit feed.

## 🛠️ Troubleshooting Common Issues

If something isn't working as expected, don't panic. Here are quick fixes for the most common hiccups.

### ❌ "App Not Installed" Error

- Make sure you have **enough storage space** on your device (at least 50MB free).
- Download the APK **again**—the file might have been corrupted during download.
- Check that you're not **downgrading** from a newer version to an older one.

### 🔐 Client ID Not Working

- Double-check that you copied the **entire code**, including uppercase and lowercase letters.
- Ensure you selected **"Installed app"** when creating the Client ID (not "Web app" or "Script").
- Try repeating the process with a **fresh Client ID** if it still fails.

### 📶 Slow Loading or No Content

- Check your internet connection (Wi-Fi or mobile data).
- Restart Currents by swiping it away from your recent apps and reopening it.
- Log out and log back in under **Settings** to refresh your session.
